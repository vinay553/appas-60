A Brighter Tomorrow

Editorial · The Sunday Standard · MADRAS, November 13, 1966

Transcribed from the page-six editorial. Line breaks and end-of-line hyphenation have been normalized; the original wording is retained.

DIVALI this year was not the customary gay occasion with its accent on merrymaking, but rather a time to renew our strength for the tasks in the year ahead. Divali comes at a time when the common man faces a multitude of problems. But it is not a time for despair.

With large parts of the country facing drought conditions in U.P., Madhya Pradesh and Bihar, it would be singularly inappropriate if others remained oblivious of their agony. It is only in a carefree atmosphere that the common man can participate in festivities.

This does not mean that there is no place for laughter and enjoyment in this land, but that the time is not now. We forgo a brightened Divali so that we can preserve our energies for the tasks which await us. No task is too small, for each has his part to play in building up the prosperity of the nation. The businessman, the social reformer, the student, the factory worker and the housewife — all have their distinctive roles.

DIVALI symbolises the triumph of good over evil. The evils which India is today contending against are poverty and the lack of discipline to overcome it. If the forces of goodness are to triumph over these evils, it can only be if we muster our strength. In unity lies strength.

Strength can be of many types. It can wear many guises. Physical strength may be necessary to overcome a powerful enemy. Similarly, unity of purpose constitutes the main strength of a nation. This is particularly true of a nation which has gone through many vicissitudes and travails. Thus its character has been tested, and hardships have toughened its fibre. This is our testing time.

IF the evil of poverty is to be overcome so that all our tomorrows may be brightened, and if India is to become a nation where the lights burn with an incandescent glow, then each of us must participate in the economic battle. An economic battle is no less worth waging than a political battle.

It has now become almost a truism that political freedom remains incomplete so long as the nation does not win its economic freedom. Only with the attainment of economic freedom will the country come into its rightful heritage. We sacrifice today for a brighter tomorrow.
